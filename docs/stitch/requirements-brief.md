# BingeBox: independent frontend concept brief

Create a fresh, original web design concept for BingeBox, a movie and series discovery and viewing application. Independently choose the information architecture, composition, typography and visual storytelling. No reference screens, existing layouts, implementation files or existing design systems are supplied.

## Product requirements

- Guests can browse preview metadata, search, filter and view title details, then sign in to access account features.
- Catalogue of approximately 162 movie/series titles. Search and filters include genre, audio language, subtitles, runtime and IMDb rating, with pagination and clear empty/loading/error states. Unknown ratings or languages must remain unknown.
- Signed-in viewers have selectable adult/kids profiles, age-restricted content, continue watching, up next, genre-based recommendations and genre top tens.
- Title details need film/series metadata, available trailers, season/episode selection, playback/resume actions, and watched-only review/rating eligibility.
- Playback supports progress, resume, seeking, quality choices subject to the plan, and understandable subscription/device-limit errors.
- Plan comparison, subscription changes, renewal preference and account/device management. Payments are simulated for this course project and must be labelled accordingly.
- Staff tools cover catalogue/media administration, billing and reports. Keep these secondary to viewer discovery; propose a consistent extension to these workflows.
- Actual bundled full-length videos are labelled placeholders. Do not imply possession of licensed film assets. Use fictional titles and original or clearly illustrative visual assets for concepts. Avoid inventing unsupported product features such as social feeds, watch parties or live TV.

## User preferences

Minimal and polished, luxurious and welcoming, visually memorable and appealing. Dominant black #000000, secondary white #FFFFFF, and #2C3480 highlights. These are palette requirements, not a layout reference. Interpret the balance creatively while keeping readable text and controls.

## Motion and interaction requirements

Propose and, wherever the generated prototype supports it, implement appealing motion: scroll-wheel/trackpad-driven animation, reveal/choreography while scrolling, depth or image movement, hover/focus responses, tactile button and filter feedback, and transitions between discovery and details. Choose an original, coherent motion direction yourself rather than adding unrelated effects. Give concrete animation timings, easing and triggers in your explanation.

Keep native page scrolling, keyboard navigation, touch equivalents and obvious controls. Do not trap or hijack the scroll wheel. Respect prefers-reduced-motion with a complete usable static alternative; avoid flashing, mandatory animation delays and autoplay audio. Keep motion efficient, primarily transform/opacity, and don't conceal required information behind hover alone.

## Deliverables

Generate a high-fidelity desktop discovery screen and explain its original concept, main interactions, and proposed mobile adaptation. Include enough real product controls to judge usability, not only a decorative landing page. If supported, provide working prototype animation code; explicitly distinguish implemented animation from ideas in prose. Explain how the visual system would extend to title details, profile selection, plans and staff screens without designing every screen at once. This is design exploration, not a request to deploy or change a running application.
